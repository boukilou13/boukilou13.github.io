Hospital Bag Checklist: What to Pack for Labor and Delivery

What to pack for mom, baby and your partner, when to pack it, and a free mini checklist you can use right now.

If you're expecting your first baby, the hospital bag can feel like one more thing to figure out. Pack too much and you're dragging a suitcase around. Pack too little and you're sending your partner to the store at 2 a.m.

This guide covers what to pack for mom, baby and your partner, plus when to pack and a few things most first-time parents wish they'd known.

When should you pack your hospital bag?

Many parents aim to have the bag ready by around week 34 to 36, since babies sometimes arrive early. Ask your doctor or midwife what timing fits your situation. Keep the bag by the door or in the car, and keep your documents in one easy-to-grab folder.

What to pack for mom

  • ID and insurance card
  • Birth plan (a few printed copies)
  • Phone and a long charging cable (hospital outlets are often far from the bed)
  • Comfortable robe or nightgown
  • Non-slip socks or slippers
  • Nursing bra or comfortable bralette
  • Lip balm (hospital air is dry)
  • Toiletries: toothbrush, hair ties, face wipes
  • Going-home outfit (loose, soft, and a size or two bigger than you think)
  • Snacks and a refillable water bottle

What to pack for baby

  • Going-home outfit (pack a newborn size and a 0-3 month size)
  • Swaddle blanket
  • Hat
  • Mittens or scratch-free sleepers
  • Car seat, installed and checked before the due date

What to pack for your partner or support person

  • Change of clothes and a light jacket
  • Phone charger
  • Snacks and cash for vending machines
  • Toiletries and a pillow
  • A copy of the birth plan and the important phone numbers

Documents to keep in one folder

  • ID and insurance information
  • Hospital pre-registration papers
  • Birth plan
  • Pediatrician's name and phone number
  • Notes with questions to ask the nurses

Tips from parents who've done it

  1. Don't pack everything. The hospital provides many basics for mom and baby, so check what yours supplies.
  2. Use separate pouches for mom, baby and partner. It's much easier to find things when you're tired.
  3. Keep a "last-minute" list on the fridge: phone, charger, glasses, anything you use daily.
  4. Pack a going-home outfit in two sizes. Newborns vary a lot.
